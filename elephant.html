Von Neumann’s Elephant

“With four parameters I can fit an elephant, and with five I can make him wiggle his trunk.”
— John von Neumann, as Enrico Fermi recounted it to Freeman Dyson [1]

Table I. Parameters

p₁ = 50 − 30i×1.00
p₂ = 18 + 8i×1.00
p₃ = 12 − 10i×1.00
p₄ = −14 − 60i×1.00
p₅ = 40 + 20iRe p₅ = 0

The fifth parameter. Move it to make him wiggle. Its real part sets the trunk’s swing; its imaginary part places the eye at (20, 20). Limited to 0–15 so the curve stays smooth.

The curve

x(t) = −60 cos t + 30 sin t − 8 sin 2t + 10 sin 3t
y(t) = 50 sin t + 18 sin 2t − 12 cos 3t + 14 cos 5t

Each complex parameter p₁…p₄ carries two of the Fourier coefficients above [2] — four complex numbers, eight real ones, and out comes an elephant. Drag a slider to zero to watch a parameter’s share of the animal disappear. The wiggle curls the trunk — the loop reaching out on the right — about its attachment, with an amplitude that grows toward the tip, driven by Re p₅. The loops touching the ground are feet, and they stay planted.
